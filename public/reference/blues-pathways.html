1 — B♭ minor pentatonic + chord tones. The B♭ minor pentatonic (B♭ D♭ E♭ F A♭) stays fixed for the whole tune as your "home" shape — it's the blues sound that works over every chord. The current chord's own tones are overlaid in maroon so you can see where the harmony lands inside that one fixed shape.

2 — Pentatonic of the current chord. Built on the literal root of whichever chord is active. Dominant and diminished chords get the bright major pentatonic (e.g. B♭ major pentatonic over B♭7); minor 7 chords (Cm7) get their own minor pentatonic instead (C minor pentatonic), since a major pentatonic doesn't fit a minor chord.

3 — Bebop scale (Mixolydian + major 7). Barry Harris's core device: an 8-note scale — Mixolydian with a chromatic major-7th passing tone inserted between the ♭7 and the root — so chord tones land on strong beats when you run the scale in straight 8ths. Over Cm7 (a ii-7 heading to F7), this card borrows F mixolydian bebop — the related V7's scale — with Cm7's own chord tones (maroon) overlaid on top.

4 — Pat Martino's minor conversion (or Dorian). Dominant and diminished chords are reduced to a minor pentatonic (optionally hexatonic) built a perfect 5th above the root — e.g. F minor pentatonic over B♭7. A chord that's already minor (Cm7) has no conversion to make, so this card just shows that chord's own Dorian scale instead.

5 — Altered scale (bars 4, 8, 10 & 12 only). These bars function as strong dominants resolving down a fifth. The altered/super-Locrian scale — equivalent to the melodic minor a half-step above the root, reordered to start on the root — is shown with that same dominant's plain chord tones (1 3 5 ♭7) overlaid in maroon so you can see how the altered tensions sit around the underlying chord.

Built for practicing chord-scale relationships across jazz progressions. Concepts: Barry Harris (bebop scales & altered dominant), Pat Martino (minor conversion) & Randy Vincent (melodic cells & hexatonics).
