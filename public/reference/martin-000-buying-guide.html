1. Executive answer

The Martin 000 is the most balanced classic Martin body for a player who cares about touch, chords, fingerstyle, jazz-blues voicings, couch writing, recording, and singing accompaniment. A D-28 is the cannon. An OM is the long-scale fingerstyle athlete. A 000 is the intimate, dry, controlled, beautiful middle ground.

In Martin language, 000, pronounced “triple-oh,” is an Auditorium-sized body. It is smaller than a dreadnought, larger than a 00, and generally about comfort, balance, midrange clarity, and quick response rather than maximum volume. The classic modern 14-fret 000 usually has a 24.9 inch short scale. That lower string tension is a major part of the feel: easier bends, a softer left hand, and a slightly compressed attack compared with a long-scale OM or dreadnought.

The “big three” standard 000 choices are:

000-18

Mahogany back and sides, spruce top. Dry, woody, immediate, fundamental-heavy. Great for blues, folk, ragtime, early country, songwriting, and fingerstyle. Often the smartest player’s buy.

000-28

Rosewood back and sides, spruce top. Richer bass and overtones, more lush sustain, more “classic Martin” sparkle. Great all-rounder for singing, recording, jazzier chords, and fingerstyle.

000-42 / 000-45

Rosewood plus pearl trim and higher appointments. The tone can be spectacular, but much of the price is rarity, decoration, collectability, and association with Eric Clapton’s 1939 000-42.

Best vintage era: broadly, the golden era is late 1920s through 1944, especially 1934 to late 1944 for 14-fret steel-string 000s with light build and scalloped bracing. Best practical modern buy: a recent 000-18, 000-28, 000-28EC, 000-28 Modern Deluxe, or an Authentic/Custom Shop if you want prewar feel without vintage fragility. Best “one guitar forever” choice: a clean 000-28 or 000-18 that feels alive in your hands.

2. What “000” means

Martin’s old body-size ladder ran from very small parlor sizes up through 0, 00, 000, and later D for dreadnought. The 000 arrived as one of Martin’s larger pre-dreadnought bodies. In the early 20th century, it was a big guitar. Today it feels medium-sized: comfortable seated, easy around the shoulder, but still large enough to sound like a serious steel-string.

000 vs OM: This is the key confusion. The original OM was essentially a 14-fret 000 body with a longer 25.4 inch scale and Orchestra Model features. Modern Martin 000s are usually short scale, while OMs are usually long scale. That one difference changes the feel dramatically.

4. Construction and materials evolution

Body and scale

The canonical 14-fret 000 is a smaller, shallower-bodied Martin than a dreadnought. Most modern 000s use a 24.9 inch scale. The shorter scale reduces string tension compared with a 25.4 inch OM or dreadnought. For your playing world, jazz guitar, blues, songwriting, Dead/country/folk, and chord-melody explorations, that matters. It lets the guitar speak with less effort and makes left-hand stretches and partial bends feel friendlier.

Bracing

The brace story is the heart of vintage Martin buying. Prewar and wartime 000s with light scalloped bracing are coveted because they are responsive, loud for their size, and harmonically alive. In late 1944 Martin changed away from scalloped bracing to strengthen instruments and reduce warranty issues. Later guitars can be wonderful, but the prewar scalloped feel is different: quicker bloom, more air, more bass for the body size, and a more elastic top.

Brace featureEffectBuyer translation
Scalloped bracingLighter, more responsive top; more bass and bloom.Premium feature for vintage-style tone.
Straight/non-scalloped bracingMore controlled, durable, often tighter.Can record beautifully; usually less expensive.
Forward-shifted bracingMoves X closer to soundhole; generally more open lower bout.More power and bass. Not all 000 models use the same pattern.
Small maple bridge plateClassic light build.Desirable. Oversized bridge plates reduce value and can dampen tone.
Large rosewood bridge plateSeen on many 1970s Martins.Durable but often blamed for reduced responsiveness.

Neck joints, glue, finish, and neck shapes

Traditional higher-end Martins use a dovetail neck joint and nitrocellulose lacquer. Vintage examples used hide glue. Many modern Authentic models returned to hide glue construction to chase the old response. Neck profiles vary enormously: 1930s V necks, 1950s fuller necks, 1960s slimmer profiles, 1990s low-profile necks, modified V on the Clapton 000-28EC, and modern modified low oval/high-performance taper on many current models.

Do not buy a vintage 000 by year alone. Buy by condition, originality, neck angle, bridge/saddle health, cracks, top integrity, and whether the guitar is alive under your hands.

6. What music has the Martin 000 been used for?

Acoustic blues

Probably the 000’s spiritual home. The short scale and balanced body suit alternating bass, slides, ragtime figures, and vocal accompaniment. Mahogany 000-18s and rosewood 000-28s both work beautifully.

Folk and singer-songwriter

The 000 is less boomy than a dreadnought, so it leaves room for vocals. It records well because the low end is controlled.

Jazz-adjacent acoustic playing

For shell voicings, Freddie Green-like acoustic comping, chord melody fragments, and warm fingerstyle harmony, a 000 can be more civilized than a dreadnought.

Country and old-time

Before dreadnought dominance, smaller Martins were common working instruments. 000s handle Carter-style bass/chord playing and old-time accompaniment well.

Rock unplugged

Clapton made the 000-42 a pop-culture symbol on MTV Unplugged. The 000 became the sound of mature acoustic rock: clear, contained, and emotionally close.

Studio work

The 000’s controlled bass and articulate midrange make it a microphone-friendly guitar. It often sits in a mix with less EQ than a dreadnought.

The major 000 cultural moment: Clapton Unplugged

Clapton’s 1992 MTV Unplugged performance is the 000 family’s equivalent of a Beatles-on-Ed-Sullivan moment. The album included “Layla” and “Tears in Heaven,” won major Grammys, sold in enormous numbers, and showed millions of electric guitar players that a small-bodied Martin could be a serious lead/vocal instrument rather than a polite folk accessory. The guitar most tied to that image is the 1939 000-42.

First decision: 000-18 or 000-28?

Clapton vibe000-28EC or 000-42That is the visual and tonal neighborhood.

Second decision: standard, vintage, or custom?

Buy new/current

Best if you want warranty, stability, modern playability, and no vintage drama. Start with current 000-18 and 000-28.

Buy used modern

Best value zone. Look at 000-28EC, 000-18GE, 000-28VS, Marquis, Modern Deluxe, and dealer Custom Shop runs.

Buy vintage

Best if the story, old wood, and collectability matter. Budget for a luthier inspection and repairs.

Third decision: neck shape

This is not a side issue. It may decide the whole purchase. Clapton 000-28EC models use a modified V feel that some players adore and some find fatiguing. Authentic and Golden Era models may have larger vintage necks. Standard modern 000s often feel easier to many electric players.

Fourth decision: 000 or OM?

Because you play jazz guitar and care about harmony, you should try a 000-28 against an OM-28. The OM has more tension and articulation. The 000 has more give and sweetness. For flatpicked fiddle tunes and complex fingerstyle, OM may win. For singing, blues, couch writing, and relaxed chord work, 000 may win.

12. Vintage inspection checklist

  • Neck angle: Is there enough saddle height? Does it need a neck reset?
  • Bridge originality: Original bridge? Shaved? Oversized replacement? Correct footprint?
  • Bridge plate: Original small maple plate or enlarged/replaced plate?
  • Top condition: Belly, sinkage around soundhole, cracks, loose braces, distortion.
  • Cracks: Top, back, sides, pickguard crack, center seam, headstock repairs.
  • Finish: Original finish, overspray, refinish, touch-up. Refinish can dramatically reduce value.
  • Braces: Loose, shaved, replaced, reglued properly?
  • Neck: Original profile, reset history, refret quality, truss rod era, relief.
  • Nut width and string spacing: Make sure it fits your hand. Specs changed across eras.
  • Tuners: Original tuners matter to collectors; replacements may be fine for players.
  • Playability: Action at 12th fret, fret wear, intonation, buzzes, dead spots.
  • Paper trail: Reputable dealer, return policy, luthier report, provenance.
  • CITES/Brazilian rosewood: Important for international travel/sale of Brazilian rosewood guitars.

Rule: Any vintage 000 over serious money should be inspected by a Martin-experienced luthier before the return window closes. The most expensive phrase in vintage guitars is “I think it’s all original.”

13. Context: Where the 000 sits in the Martin family and against its great rivals

The 000 is not simply a smaller dreadnought. It is Martin's balanced, professional small-body voice: more grown-up and versatile than a parlor, less forceful than a dreadnought, softer under the left hand than an OM, and often easier to record than almost anything else. If the dreadnought is the bluegrass cannon and the OM is the articulate fingerstyle machine, the 000 is the singer, writer, blues player, jazz-chord player, and living-room heirloom.

The key decision: 000 versus OM

The OM is the nearest relative and the one you absolutely need to audition beside a 000. Martin groups 000 and OM together as Auditorium bodies in its current body-size taxonomy, but the traditional buying distinction is scale length and feel. Current Martin 000-28 specs list a 24.9 inch scale length, while current OM-28 specs list a 25.4 inch scale length. That half-inch difference changes the guitar more than the spec sheet makes it sound.

Practical test: Play the same first-position G, a ragtime alternating-bass pattern in C, a blues in E, a barred F, a fingerstyle piece above the 7th fret, and then your jazz shell voicings. If the OM makes you play better, buy the OM. If the 000 makes you want to keep playing for an hour, buy the 000.

Gibson and the great alternative tradition

Martin and Gibson split the American flat-top imagination. Martin tends to be associated with balance, fundamental acoustic purity, rosewood dreadnought authority, and the 000/OM small-body lineage. Gibson's great acoustics are often more midrangey, thumpy, dry, compressed, and singer-friendly. A Martin 000 can sound like polished wood and air. A good Gibson can sound like a record before you even put a microphone in front of it.

How to think about competitors when you shop

Do not buy by brand alone

A mediocre Martin is not better than a great Collings, Santa Cruz, or Gibson. Play the individual guitar.

Decide whether you want polish or character

Martin 000s are balanced and elegant. Gibsons can be more flawed and more charismatic. Boutique guitars can be cleaner and more consistent.

Separate guitar tone from record mythology

Clapton's Unplugged tone is partly guitar, but also player, mic, room, arrangement, and nostalgia.

Let your hands vote

Scale length, neck shape, nut width, and string spacing will matter more after two hours than pearl trim ever will.
