Part 0The Network Model

One mental model runs through this entire system. Learn it first; every drill afterward is just a region of this map.

A triad network is a graph. The nodes are harmonic objects: triads, 7th arpeggios, quartal stacks, pentatonic fragments, dim7 arpeggios. The edges are the moves that connect two nodes: chromatic enclosures, passing diminished, guide-tone steps, pentatonic connectors, chromatic slides. A triad pair is the smallest possible network: two nodes, no edges. That is exactly why raw pair playing sounds like an exercise. The pairs are consonant blocks separated by a whole step, with none of the half-step gravity that defines bebop. This system fixes that by making you practice edges as deliberately as you practice nodes. It is also why enclosures are conceptually prior to pairs here: learn the gravity mechanism first, then the blocks that lack it, and the blocks never get the chance to sound like blocks.

Inside vs outside is graph distance. A node built from chord tones is distance zero. Diatonic nodes and edges are one step out. Chromatic edges add tension that resolves by half step, so they sound out for a beat and in on arrival. Superimposed systems (melodic minor, quartal) are further out, and planing or side-slipping the whole network is the edge of the map. The level system below is just this distance made explicit, and it matches the five-level complexity ladder in Line Lab, so the guide and the app grade material the same way.

The one-sentence method Practice nodes until they are automatic, then practice edges until the seams disappear, then displace everything rhythmically until it stops sounding like geometry and starts sounding like language.

Part 0.5The Bebop Skeleton

Before any device: the metric grammar everything must fit. Bebop is not a scale sound, it is targeted motion through harmony. Every later object in this system, pair, cell, pivot, enclosure, is judged by whether it obeys this skeleton.

  • Chord tones on strong beats (1 and 3), approach material on weak beats and upbeats. The listener's ear checks the harmony on the strong beats; that is where you must be accountable.
  • Guide-tone rails. The 3rds and 7ths are the destination grid. Over a II-V-I: b7 of the II resolves to 3 of the V, b7 of the V resolves to 3 of the I. Every phrase you build later decorates this rail.
  • Resolution into the next chord, not just the current one. A phrase that ends where the next harmony begins is a sentence; one that ends inside its own chord is a fragment.
  • Short phrases first. Two-beat and one-bar phrases with eighth-note flow, before anything longer.

Starter vocabulary, deliberately tiny: 1-3-5-7, diatonic passing tones, one chromatic approach, one enclosure. Drill: over a looped II-V-I, build two-beat phrases that land a guide tone on beat 1 of every chord, using nothing else. When that is automatic, the skeleton is installed and the devices can hang on it.

Part 1The Level System: Inside to Outside

Each level defines which nodes and edges you are allowed to use. Restriction is the point: you cannot hear what a level sounds like if you are always allowed everything. Every level has a gate; do not move up until you clear it.

LevelAllowed nodesAllowed edgesSound reference
L1Chord tones Arpeggio of the chord itself; cells 1-2-3-5 and 3-5-7-9 None. Blocks only. Swing-era vertical playing
L2Diatonic The classic pairs (C+D, Eb+F, Bb+C); diatonic 7th arps Diatonic pivots only: Em7, Gmaj7, Am7 upper structure Early modal, inside modern
L3Chromatic gravity Same nodes as L2 Enclosures, passing diminished, neighbor expansion, chromatic approach Parker, Barry Harris
L4Superimposition Melodic minor systems (lyd. dom., alt, m7b5), quartal stacks, pentatonic fragments All of L3 plus quartal and pentatonic pivots Shorter, Hancock, Stern
L5Outside Alt pairs (Gb+Ab), symmetric dim systems (C, Eb, Gb, A), superimposed II-V structures, any node a half step away Chromatic planing, side-slipping the whole network, symmetric sequencing Brecker, Rosenwinkel, Monder

Gates. L1 all four cell rotations from every chord tone, forward and retrograde, 80 bpm, no stopping. L2 one pair in all inversions through one position, plus one pivot chain, from memory. L3 every triad arrival in a chorus approached by enclosure or half step, recorded and checked. L4 the C+D pair deployed over Cmaj7#11, C7#11, and Gb7alt in one sitting (the transferability test). L5 leave and return convincingly: one bar out, resolve by half step, on a recording you would let someone else hear.

Part 2Device Modules

Five devices, one identical practice loop: Hear → Isolate → Connect → Displace → Apply. The loop is the method; the device is just the material fed through it. Two rules bind every drill: sing the target notes before you play them, and end on a named resolution note. If you cannot name where the phrase lands, you are running a fingering, not language. Default protocol for every module: metronome 60 to 120 bpm in steps of 10, three keys per day moving in fourths, one fretboard position per week, record 8 bars at the end of each week.

Module A · Triad Pairs (nodes)

Two triads, no common tones, alternated in inversions. The raw material.

Hear

Sing both triads over a droned root, then sing only the color tones (F# and A over C) so the ear owns the tension before the fingers do.

Isolate

One pair, one position. Root position, 1st, and 2nd inversions of each triad, ascending and descending: C E G · D F# A, then E G C · F# A D, then G C E · A D F#. Then interlock: up C, down D, up C in the next inversion. Then extract 4-note cells from the combined collection: C E D F#, E G F# A, G C A D. Six-note streams are the exercise sound; four-note extractions are the language sound.

Connect

Voice-led inversion chains chosen for minimal motion: E G C → F# A D (every voice moves a whole step), and G C E → F# A D (one voice falls while two rise). Hear voices, not blocks.

Displace

Start the second triad on the and of 1, then on beat 2, then on the and of 2. Same notes, three different phrases.

Apply

Loop one chord from the reference table (Cmaj7 → C+D, Cm7 → Eb+F, C7 → Bb+C) and improvise using only that pair for 3 minutes. Subordination rule: over Cmaj7, land E or B on strong beats, treat F# and A as color, resolve D to C or E when the phrase ends. The collection serves the harmony, never the reverse.

Priority pairs, in order: C+D over maj7#11 (also covers C7#11 and Gb7alt), Eb+F over m7 dorian, Bb+C over dom7, F+G over tonic melodic minor, Gb+Ab over alt.

Module B · Melodic Cells (nodes)

Four-note units you sequence and displace. The bebop atom.

Hear

Sing each cell from its chord tone, then sing only the destination note of the phrase you are about to play.

Isolate

Core cells over C: 1-2-3-5 (Coltrane cell), 3-5-7-9 (rootless arp), 5-6-7-9, 1-2-b3-5 minor, b9-#9-3-b7 (alt cell). Run each from every chord tone, forward and retrograde.

Connect

Chain cells so the last note of one is a half step or whole step from the first note of the next. Sequence 1-2-3-5 up the scale degrees, then through the cycle of fourths.

Displace

The card's own rule, made a drill: shift each cell to start on the upbeat. Then start mid-cell (note 3 on beat 1). A four-note unit sounds like language only after it survives displacement.

Apply

Over a II-V loop, one cell per chord, changing the starting chord tone every chorus.

Key identity: 3-5-7-9 is the money cell. Em7 arp over C is Cmaj9; Ebmaj7 arp over Cm is Cm9; Em7b5 arp over C7 is C9. One shape, three chords.

Module C · Pivot Arpeggios (edges as objects)

An intermediate arpeggio with maximum overlap with both triads. The A-X-B move.

Hear

Sing triad A's last note, the pivot's outline, and triad B's first note as one line. If you cannot sing the seam, the pivot is decoration.

Isolate

Learn each pivot as a shape between C and D: Em7 (E G B D) smooth and inside, Gmaj7 (G B D F#) linear and modern, C#°7 (C# E G Bb) bebop tension, quartal E A D open, A7 (A C# E G) dominant, tonicizing the destination, B° (B D F) Barry Harris symmetric, Db triad chromatic slide.

Connect

The reinterpretation question, as a drill: pick the last note of triad A and the first note of triad B, and find three different arpeggios containing both. For G → D: G major, Em, Bm. Play all three; hear three flavors of the same seam. Every rep uses the full format: primary object, pivot, target object, named resolution note. Example: C triad, C#°7 fragment, D triad, resolve to E, the 3rd of Cmaj7.

Displace

Vary where the pivot lands: pivot on beat 2, pivot straddling the barline, pivot compressed into a triplet (see Module E).

Apply

One chorus where every triad change goes through a pivot, and no pivot type repeats within 8 bars.

Litmus test: the Gmaj7 pivot proves the concept. C E G · G B D F# · D F# A is not a jump at all, it is one continuous lydian stack: C E G B D F# A. Outside pivots (Dbmaj7, Ebm7, even Ebm7 to Ab7 as a superimposed II-V into D) are judged by one criterion only: the strength of the resolution, never membership in the parent scale.

Module D · Chromatic Enclosures (edges)

Approach every arrival from above and below. This is the half-step gravity the pairs lack.

Hear

Sing the target note first, then the enclosure into it. The ear should want the resolution before the hands supply it.

Isolate

Four enclosure shapes on a single target (D): above-below Eb C# D, below-above C# Eb D, double chromatic from below C C# D, scale-above chromatic-below E C# D. All four on every note of both triads in the pair. Grade them by size: single approach (C# D), two-note enclosure (Eb C# D), extended three-note (Eb C C# D). Bigger enclosures buy more tension and cost more time; spend accordingly.

Connect

Enclose only the first note of each new triad: C E G · Eb C# D · F# A. Then neighbor expansion, where every triad tone gets its own approach: Eb-D · G-F# · Bb-A. Then the passing diminished as a four-note enclosure of the entire triad: C E G · C# E G Bb · D F# A, every voice resolving by half step.

Displace

Move the enclosure off the downbeat so the target lands on the beat and the tension lands before it. Enclosure on the and of 4 into a downbeat arrival is the single most Parker-sounding rhythm available.

Apply

Take yesterday's L2 recording and re-play it note for note, adding enclosures at every triad arrival. Same skeleton, different century.

Rule of thumb: only one note has to move chromatically for a pair to stop sounding like a pair. C → C# → D beats C → D every time.

Module E · Rest-Stroke Triplet Arpeggios (the engine)

Not in the source card, and it turns out to be the structural glue: every node is a 3-note shape, and a bridge (triad, connector, triad) is 9 notes, exactly three triplet beats.

Hear

Sing the triplet stream with accents on object boundaries, then sing it flat with no accents. Both versions live in the ear before the pick moves.

Isolate

Mechanics first, on the easiest material. Rest stroke: the downstroke follows through and comes to rest on the next lower string. It produces a fat accent and a physical time anchor. Rule: every string change to a new string gets a rest-stroke downstroke; notes repeating on a string alternate. One triad, three inversions up the neck, straight triplets at 60 bpm, rest stroke on the first note of every beat. Then restart the same material from the second and third triplet partials.

Connect

Pair alternation on the triplet grid: C E G on beat 1, D F# A on beat 2, next inversions on beats 3 and 4. Then the 9-note bridge as three triplet beats: triad A, connector, triad B, with the rest-stroke accent announcing each object. That accent is stage one, training wheels for the ear.

Displace

Stage two: erase the accents. Same stream, phrased across the barline with no bump at object boundaries, so the line stops telegraphing its construction. Then start the 9-note bridge on beat 2 so it ends on the and of 4. Then superimpose 3 against 4: continuous triplets while the pair changes every 4 notes, so the accents drift across the shapes. This is where Martino-style relentlessness comes from.

Apply

One chorus of continuous triplets over a slow loop, all vocabulary drawn from the current level's nodes and edges. Sparse comping, metronome on 2 and 4.

Why it matters: the triplet grid removes the decision of when to play, so all attention goes to which node and which edge. It is a constraint that manufactures fluency.

Lesson scoping One session, one of each: one harmonic context, one pair, one cell, one pivot type, one enclosure type, one rhythmic pattern, one final phrase, one outside level. Next session, keep everything and change exactly one element. Depth comes from controlled variation, not from new material.

Part 3The Bridge Practicum

The rotating-connector drill from the source card, expanded into a six-week routine. The exercise: never play A B, A B, A B. Always play A, connector, B · B, connector, A, and force a different connector every single time. After a few weeks the brain stops hearing two triads and starts hearing one melodic line that happens to be built from pair vocabulary.

The Phrase Formula (week 6 capstone)

The full sentence grammar the bridges have been building toward:

Grow the chain. Start with three components, add one per week: cell → pivot → target
enclosure → cell → pivot → target
pickup → enclosure → cell → triad fragment → pivot → second triad → chromatic exit → guide-tone resolution

Not every phrase needs every element; the formula is a menu, not a mandate. A worked example over Cmaj7 heading into Dm7:

One phrase, five components: C# D · E G F# A · G B D F# · E · F A C

C# D chromatic approach, E G F# A interlocked C/D pair material, G B D F# Gmaj7 pivot, E the resolution note bridging both harmonies, F A C Dm7 arrival. This analysis format is exactly what Line Lab should emit next to every generated line.

Part 4Progression Types

The network deployed against the six harmonic situations that cover most of the repertoire. Each one stresses a different part of the system: fast harmonic rhythm stresses cells, static harmony stresses edges and levels, cyclic dominants stress transferability.

Major II-V-I (C)

The one-pair rail, then the half-step drop: Dm7  → F + G   (dorian pair)
G7   → F + G   (same pair, reinterpreted as b7 + 1)
G7alt → Db + Eb (Ab mel. minor)
Cmaj7 → C + D   (lydian pair)

Two insights do all the work here. First, F + G covers both Dm7 and G7, so the first three bars can ride one rail and the line stays coherent by construction. Second, the alt pair Db + Eb resolves into the tonic pair C + D by parallel half-step planing: the outside move and the resolution are the same physical shape a fret apart. That is L5 tension with L1 logic.

Minor II-V-i (C minor)

Three parallel melodic minor systems: Dm7b5 → F mel. minor  → Bb + C
G7alt  → Ab mel. minor → Db + Eb
Cm(maj7) → C mel. minor  → F + G

The entire minor II-V-i is one device, melodic minor, transposed twice: up a minor third, then up a major third. Any melodic minor vocabulary you own transposes directly through all three chords. The pairs even move logically: Bb+C → Db+Eb → F+G, two half-step-adjacent systems resolving up a fourth. Practice the three scales as one gesture before practicing the pairs.

Static chords (one-chord vamps)

No harmonic rhythm means you supply the motion. This is where the level system becomes the form itself. Over a Cmaj7 vamp: 8 bars L1 (pure arps and cells), 8 bars L2 (C+D with diatonic pivots), 8 bars L3 (every arrival enclosed), 8 bars L4 (quartal and pentatonic pivots), 8 bars L5 (side-slip to Db+Eb and resolve). Then freely mix, with a tension budget: you may leave whenever you like, but every departure must return by half step within two beats. Static harmony is the only context where you can hear each level in isolation, which is why it comes before tunes.

Modal (D dorian, So What / Impressions form)

Nodes: the dorian pair F + G, the minor pair Dm + Em, quartal stacks D G C and E A D. Sixteen bars is a long time; structure it as a network walk, not a scale run. The AABA form gives you the outside move for free: when the form lifts to Eb dorian, everything transposes up a half step (Gb + Ab, Ebm + Fm). Practice deliberately blurring that seam, starting the Eb material a bar early (L5 side-slip) or holding D material a bar late, then resolving.

Rhythm changes (Bb)

The A sections move at two beats per chord, too fast for pairs. This is cell territory: one 4-note cell per chord, glued by guide tones. 1-2-3-5 on Bb, 3-5-b7-9 on G7, and so on, with the 3rds and 7ths as the rail the cells decorate. The bridge is the opposite: one chord per two bars, exactly one pair per chord, and the dominant pairs descend the cycle in a perfect sequence:

Bridge: one pair per dominant, moving in fourths: D7 → C + D   ·  G7 → F + G   ·  C7 → Bb + C   ·  F7 → Eb + F

The same b7+1 shape transposed four times. Learn the bridge as one object and rhythm changes stops being scary. Drill: A sections at L1–L3 (cells and enclosures), bridge at L2–L4 (pairs and pivots), and alternate which section gets the attention each day.

Weekly protocol on the loop

  1. Mon/Tue: passes L1 and L2 at 60 percent tempo, three keys is waived, this stays in A minor.
  2. Wed/Thu: pass L3, plus the week's Bridge Practicum connector family applied to the tune.
  3. Fri: passes L4 and L5, triplet-grid versions of anything that survived the week.
  4. Sat: record one full chorus, free level mixing with the tension budget. Audit against last week's take: fewer seams, or no promotion. Then compose: write and record three original phrases over bars 1 to 8, one fully inside (L1 to L2), one chromatic (L3), one deliberately outside with a clear resolution (L5). Improvising rents the vocabulary; composing buys it.
Endgame The tutorial succeeds when the vocabulary stops being nameable in real time. You will not think "Em7 pivot into an enclosure on the triplet grid." You will think in regions of the network and hear one continuous line, which is what Brecker, Rosenwinkel, Lund, and Monder are doing when triad pairs sound like language instead of geometry.

Part 6Martino Mode: The One-Region II-V-I

Your signature reduction. Three intimidating moves, ii minor over the ii, ii minor over the dominant, lydian on the tonic, collapse into one thing you already own: a single minor region and a single note flip. This is the section that makes the hard stuff easy, because it removes almost every decision.

The whole idea in one line Dm7, G7, and Cmaj7 in C major are the same seven notes. Stay in D minor across the ii and the V. Flip one note (F to F#) on the I. That is Martino Mode.

Why it works: you are refusing to move

D dorian, G mixolydian, and C major are one scale spelled from three starting points. So "ii minor over the ii" and "ii minor over the dominant" are not two techniques. They are you playing D minor and declining to change when the chord changes. Over G, that same D-minor material reads as a rootless G9 (D-F-A = 5, b7, 9); let the full scale through and the 3rd (B) and 13th (E) are already under your fingers as G mixolydian. The lydian on the I is then a one-note flip: raise F to F#, and D dorian becomes D mixolydian, which is C lydian. Natural F for the changes, sharp F the instant you land on the tonic.

Network 1 · The Changes (Dm7 and G7): C major / D dorian

One region covers both chords. Everything here is diatonic to C. The minor conversion is the whole point: Dm is your answer to both bars.

Network 3 · The Altered Detour (G7alt): Ab melodic minor

When you want teeth on the V, this is the only region that leaves home, and it leaves by exactly a half step. G7alt is Ab melodic minor, sitting one fret above your C-lydian region. The trick that makes it easy: G7 and G7alt share the B-F tritone. Keep those two notes as anchors, add the altered color around them, then slide the whole shape down a half step into C+D.

Make it easy: the five-drill ladder

Each drill isolates exactly one thing. Do them in order; do not add the next element until the current one is automatic. This is how a hard sound becomes a reflex.

L1Drill 1 · Anchor the tritone

The skeleton. Three notes for the whole progression.

Over the loop, play only guide tones: F on Dm7 (b3), B or F on G7 (3 or b7), E on Cmaj7 (3). Half notes, metronome on 2 and 4. You are proving you can hear the resolution with three notes before you decorate anything.

L2Drill 2 · The one-region flip

The core move, nothing else.

Noodle freely in D dorian over Dm7 and G7. On beat 1 of Cmaj7, change exactly one note: F becomes F#. Everything else stays. Loop it slow until the flip is muscle memory and you stop thinking about the chords at all.

L2Drill 3 · Minor-conversion licks

Prove the reduction with real vocabulary.

Take one Dm7 lick you already know. Play it verbatim over both Dm7 and G7. Hear it recolor into G9 without you touching it. Then resolve with one Bm7 lick over Cmaj7 for the lydian. Two licks, whole progression.

L3Drill 4 · Two pairs, with gravity

Add half-step motion.

F + G pair over the ii-V, C + D pair on the I. Then enclose every arrival: chromatic approach into F on Dm7, into B or F on G7, into E on Cmaj7. Same skeleton as Drill 1, now wearing bebop clothes.

L5Drill 5 · The altered lift-and-drop

The one that sounds impossible and is not.

Over G7, start in D dorian (inside). On beat 3, jump the color up a half step into Ab melodic minor (Db + Eb, or Bbm), keeping B and F under your ears as anchors. Then slide the whole shape down a half step into C + D on the tonic. Up for tension, down to resolve. That descent is the entire trick.

Level map for Martino Mode

Make it yours The region concept transposes to any key by one relationship: play the ii minor, hold it through the V, flip the 4th to #4 on the I. For altered, lift a half step above the tonic into melodic minor and slide back down. Learn it in C, then move the whole picture. The signature is not the notes, it is the refusal to move until the tonic, and the single flip when you arrive.
