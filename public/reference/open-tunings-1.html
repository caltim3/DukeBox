Tuning 01

Open D

D A D F# A D
6th 5th 4th 3rd 2nd 1st
Delta Blues Slide Guitar Folk Acoustic Rock Bottleneck
Stage Hack — From Standard Tuning
16th E→D(down whole step)
23rd G→F#(down half step)
32nd B→A(down whole step)
41st E→D(down whole step)
Why it Works

Open D forms a D major triad across all six strings, making a bar with one finger produce any major chord up the neck. It's the quintessential slide/bottleneck tuning — the lowered bass adds resonant depth, and the F# on the 3rd string gives the tuning its characteristic warm, full voice. The unison D on strings 1 and 6 creates an octave drone that rings through any chord shape, which is why Delta bluesman like Elmore James and Robert Johnson lived in this tuning.

Tuning 02

Open G

6th 5th 4th 3rd 2nd 1st

16th E→D(down whole step)

Why it Works

Open G is the Rolling Stones' secret weapon — Keith Richards removes the 6th string entirely (or just mutes it) to play a 5-string "banjo-style" rhythm guitar. Strings 2–4 remain identical to standard tuning, so chord shapes you already know on those strings still work. The open chord's G–D drone rings beneath everything. Because most rock and blues is in G, you're instantly in the home key with zero fingers on the fretboard. Slide playing shines here too, with the major chord lining up perfectly at every 5-fret interval.

Tuning 03

Open E

E B→ E G# B E
6th 5th 4th 3rd 2nd 1st
Blues Rock Slide Country Blues Gospel
Stage Hack — From Standard Tuning
15th A→B(up whole step — careful!)
24th D→E(up whole step)
33rd G→G#(up half step)
Why it Works

Open E is essentially Open D with a capo at the 2nd fret — and that's exactly how many players use it. The advantage: strings 1, 2, and 6 stay at standard pitch, so you keep your familiar high-E and B string feel. The drawback is the tuning UP approach on strings 3–5, which puts more tension on the neck — be cautious with vintage guitars. Duane Allman used Open E almost exclusively for his bottleneck work, and it's the tuning behind "Layla." The open chord rings gloriously full across all registers.

Tuning 04

Open A

E A E A C# E
6th 5th 4th 3rd 2nd 1st
Country Blues Piedmont Slide Ragtime Folk
Stage Hack — From Standard Tuning
14th D→E(up whole step)
23rd G→A(up whole step)
32nd B→C#(up whole step)
Why it Works

Open A is Open G raised a whole step — meaning you can use every Open G chord shape, just two frets higher. It retains the outer E strings of standard tuning, giving a familiar feel while transforming the inner strings. Popularized by Mississippi Fred McDowell and Son House, it's perfect for Piedmont-style thumb-and-finger ragtime patterns. The major third (C#) on the 2nd string creates the brightest, most uplifting sound of all the open major tunings — it practically begs to be strummed hard.

Tuning 05

DADGAD

D A D G A D
6th 5th 4th 3rd 2nd 1st
Celtic / Folk Fingerstyle Middle Eastern New Age Led Zeppelin
Stage Hack — From Standard Tuning
16th E→D(down whole step)
22nd B→A(down whole step)
31st E→D(down whole step)
Why it Works

DADGAD is a suspended chord tuning — no third, so it's neither major nor minor. This ambiguity is its superpower: it sounds modal and ancient, perfect for Celtic reels, Indian-influenced fingerstyle, and anything that needs to breathe and resonate. Jimmy Page discovered it in Morocco and used it to write "Kashmir." The D–A–D octave frame creates a powerful drone, and the middle strings (A–D–G) remain standard, meaning your existing scale knowledge transfers directly. Guitarists like Pierre Bensusan built entire careers on DADGAD's unique harmonic landscape.

Tuning 06

Open C

C G C G C E
6th 5th 4th 3rd 2nd 1st
Fingerstyle Alternative Rock New Age Experimental Psychedelic
Stage Hack — From Standard Tuning
16th E→C(down major 3rd)
24th D→C(down whole step)
32nd B→C(up half step)
Why it Works

Open C is the lowest, most spacious of the major open tunings — the bass C on string 6 descends a full major third below standard E, producing an enormous resonant rumble. Joni Mitchell, Nick Drake, and John Fahey all used variants of Open C for its orchestral depth. The doubled Cs (strings 6, 4, 2) create a hollow, bell-like chord that rings for days. John Fahey turned it into a fingerpicking universe; Joni Mitchell used it for dense, jazz-informed voicings that are impossible in standard tuning. Its width — nearly two octaves from low C to high E — rewards slow, meditative fingerpicking.

Tuning 07

Open D Minor

D A D F A D
6th 5th 4th 3rd 2nd 1st
Folk Psychedelic Dark Fingerstyle Ambient Classical-influenced
Stage Hack — From Standard Tuning
16th E→D(down whole step)
23rd G→F(down whole step)
31st E→D(down whole step)
Why it Works

Open Dm is identical to Open D but with the 3rd string tuned to F instead of F# — swapping the major third for a minor third changes the entire emotional character. This single-semitone shift transforms bright and country-esque into dark, melancholic, and ancient. Joni Mitchell used this tuning extensively (she called it "Joni's tuning"), and it's central to Nick Drake's work. The Dm chord as the open voice means every slide position produces a minor chord — perfect for Spanish-influenced or Moorish-sounding music. The tuning is also just two changes from Open D, making it an easy on-stage pivot.
